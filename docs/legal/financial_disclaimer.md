## 1. Calculators Are Mathematical Estimates Only

FeroCalc provides tools for estimating returns and amortization on various financial instruments available in India, including:
- Fixed Deposits (FD) & Special Tenure Schemes
- Recurring Deposits (RD)
- Systematic Investment Plans (SIP) & Mutual Fund Return Projections
- Equated Monthly Installments (EMI) & Loan Eligibility
- Public Provident Fund (PPF) & Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY)
- Compound Interest, Inflation & Capital Gains

### Key Calculation Variances:
1. **Compounding Frequencies:** Bank FDs in India typically compound interest on a quarterly basis. Different institutions may apply slight variations in day-count conventions (actual/365 vs 365/365 vs leap year 366 days).
2. **Broken Period Interest:** Initial broken periods (fractional months) before quarterly compounding cycles commence may result in minor differences between app projections and exact bank passbooks.
3. **Premature Withdrawal Penalties:** Early encashment of FDs generally incurs a penalty (typically 0.50% to 1.00% reduction in applicable contracted rates) and recalculation at the tenure actually run.
4. **Estimates Only:** Outputs are mathematical illustrations and do not guarantee actual returns, credit approval, or binding interest payout contracts.

## 4. Senior Citizen Slabs & Special Tenures

- **Age Requirement & Bank Terms:** Senior citizen interest premiums (typically +0.50% p.a., or higher on special schemes like SBI We-Care or super senior slabs) generally require resident Indian individuals to be 60 years or older. Senior citizen rates are not universally applicable to all deposit products, foreign currency deposits, or non-resident accounts; exact eligibility, tenure availability, and qualifying criteria are governed strictly by each respective bank's official terms and product circulars.
- **Documentation:** Banks mandate submission of officially valid KYC documents (PAN, Aadhaar, Passport, Voter ID) demonstrating date of birth to apply senior citizen rates.
- **Special Scheme Windows:** Special schemes (e.g., specific day-count schemes like 400 Days, 444 Days, 501 Days, 701 Days) often have limited validity windows and may close without notice.

## 5. Deposit Insurance (DICGC Coverage)

In India, deposits in eligible registered insured scheduled commercial banks, small finance banks, and eligible cooperative banks are insured by the **Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation (DICGC)**, a wholly owned subsidiary of the Reserve Bank of India:
- **Maximum Insured Limit:** ₹5,00,000 (Rupees Five Lakh) per depositor across all branches of each insured bank in the same right and capacity (combined principal and accrued interest).
- **Separate Entity Coverage & Aggregation:** Deposits held in separate, legally distinct insured banking entities are each independently insured up to the statutory ₹5 Lakh limit. Deposits held in different branches of the same banking entity are aggregated together under a single ₹5 Lakh limit. Certain deposits (such as state/central government deposits, inter-bank deposits, or foreign government deposits) are statutorily excluded from DICGC coverage.

## 6. Taxation and TDS Disclosures

- **Taxability of Interest:** Fixed deposit (FD) and recurring deposit (RD) interest is taxable under applicable Indian income tax law under the head "Income from Other Sources" (or the applicable statutory head) at the depositor's marginal income tax slab rate.
- **TDS Is Withholding, Not Tax Exemption:** Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) is strictly an advance tax collection mechanism administered by banking entities. Crossing or staying below statutory TDS thresholds does **not** determine whether interest is exempt from income tax; interest earned below withholding thresholds remains part of the depositor's total taxable income and must be declared in annual income tax returns.
- **Statutory Framework and Applicable Effective Dates:**
  - **Income-tax Act, 2025 (Effective from 1 April 2026):** For payments or credits on or after 1 April 2026, TDS on interest (other than interest on securities) paid by a banking company, qualifying co-operative bank, or applicable post-office scheme is governed by **Section 393(1), Table Serial No. 5(ii)** of the Income-tax Act, 2025. Under these provisions, the statutory annual interest withholding thresholds per financial year across all branches of a banking institution are:
    - **Resident Senior Citizens (60 years or older):** ₹1,00,000 in a financial year.
    - **Other Individuals (regular depositors):** ₹50,000 in a financial year.
  - **Earlier Statutory Regime (up to 31 March 2026):** For interest paid or credited up to 31 March 2026 governed by Section 194A of the Income-tax Act, 1961, the statutory annual withholding thresholds across all branches of a bank were ₹50,000 for resident senior citizens and ₹40,000 for other individuals.
- **PAN Requirements & Withholding Rates:**
  - Where a valid Permanent Account Number (PAN) is furnished to the deducting bank, TDS is deducted at the standard statutory withholding rate prescribed by the Income Tax Department.
  - If a valid PAN is not furnished (or in cases of specified non-compliance under governing statutory provisions), banks are legally mandated to deduct tax at higher withholding rates as specified under applicable income tax rules.
- **Self-Declaration via Form 15G / Form 15H:**
  - Eligible resident individuals whose estimated total income for the financial year is below the basic tax exemption limit may submit Form 15G (for individuals below 60 years of age) or Form 15H (for resident senior citizens aged 60 and above) to the bank to request non-deduction of TDS, subject to the conditions and eligibility criteria prescribed under official Income Tax Department rules (`incometax.gov.in`).
- **Pre-Tax Computations in FeroCalc:** FeroCalc calculations display gross pre-tax figures unless calculated explicitly within dedicated tax calculation tools.
